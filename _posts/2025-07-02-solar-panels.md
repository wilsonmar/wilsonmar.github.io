The orange line reflect the power generated by standard single-sided panels positioned at the traditional fixed 30 degree angle.

The dark-blue line reflects Bifacial panels positioned at a fixed 30 degrees slant, which captures sunlight reflected from the ground. After snowfall, the panels would capture more power. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xD1MT-ek05w">VIDEO</a>:
Static reflectors on the groud reflecting sunlight to the solar panels increase yield about 8%.

Traditional placement of panels angled toward the sun would generate the most power at mid-day.

The green line and light-blue line shows the output from <strong>bifacial</strong> panels <strong>vertically</strong> like a fence.

Such placement enable the land to be used for agriculture. And they don't need to be cleaned as often.

However, there is greater risk of theft when plated on the ground.

Because the panels do not face the sun, they do not yield as much as panels that do face the sun.

However, the power they generate produce power to the pattern of power needed -- in a "duck" pattern where highest need early morning when people get ready for the day and at the end of the day.
