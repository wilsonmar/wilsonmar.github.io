## Why Google?

1.  On <a target="_blank" href="https://wilsonmar.github.io/cloud-services-comparisons/#cloud-vendor-comparisons">being #3 among CSPs</a>: Due to fewer people working on GCP, individual professionals are likely to be paid better than AWS & Azure pros.

2.  Visit Google Cloud's marketing home page at:

    <a target="_blank" href="https://cloud.google.com/"><strong>https://cloud.google.com</strong></a>

    Google is based in Mountain View, California ("Silicon Valley").
    It became big since its search engine (appearing in 1996) and associated advertising catapulted them to be one of the most valuable companies in the world.

    Major named clients of the Google Cloud Platform (GCP) include HSBC, PayPal, 20th Century Fox, Bloomberg, Dominos, etc.

3.  Click on <a target="_blank" href="https://cloud.google.com/solutions#industry-solutions">Solutions</a> to see that Google has software specific to industries such as Retail, Healthcare, Supply Chain, even Manufacturing. Those are industries where Amazon has entered markets of its AWS cloud customers.

    Google also supports "Modernization" initiatives such as <a target="_blank" href="https://cloud.google.com/solutions/open-banking-apix">Open Banking</a>, SRE, DevOps, Day 2 Operations, Multicloud

    Google says it's the first company to be carbon-free, running on 90% carbon-free energy.

4.  On the left side of the Solutions page, <strong>>></strong> marks category names that are also in the <a href="#Products">Products menu (below)</a>:

    * Application modernization (<a href="#CICD">CI/CD</a> >> DevOps, API Management, Multicloud)
    * <a href="#AI">Artificial Intelligence</a> >>
    * APIs and applications
    * <a href="#DATABASES">Databases</a> >>
    * Data cloud
    * Digital transformation
    * Infrastructure modernization
    * Productivity and collaboration (Google Workspace, Chrome Enterprise, Cloud Identity, Cloud Search)
    * <a href="#Security">Security</a> >>
    * <a href="#BIGDATA">(Smart) Analytics</a>  >> (previously "BIG DATA")
    * Startups and SMB (Web3, Startup Program)
    * Featured partner solutions
    <br /><br />

5.  Google has innovated in its aggressive pricing among CSPs:
    * first to have per-second vs. per-minute billing.
    * discount applied automatically after an instance runs for more than 25% of a month.
    * Google encrypts data automatically at no additional charge. However, AWS charges extra for the stronger encryption for security -- an expensive hassle.
    <br /><br />

    Pricing is especially important as more usage is made of AI and Machine Learning (Vertex), which use a voracious amount of compute power.

6.  My <a target="_blank" href="https://wilsonmar.github.io/cloud-services-comparisons/#cloud-vendor-comparisons">cloud vendor comparison article</a> describes how
Google's fast fiber network of underground and undersea cables connects machines at high capacity and speed across the world.

    Even if you don't use Google Cloud, you can use Google's fast DNS at 8.8.8.8.

7.  Google was the first cloud vendor to offer a <strong>VPC which spans several regions</strong>  (until late 2018 when AWS offers the same). But still, the global scope of a VPC in Google eliminates the cost and latency of VPN between regions (plus a router for each VPN for BGP). This also enables shareable configuration between projects.

8.  As with AWS, Google has <strong>Preemptible VMs</strong> that run up to just 24 hours, with less features than Spot VMs. Both are priced the same. No "pre-warming" is required for load balancing.

9.  Largest servers? <a target="_blank" href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/google-unveils-worlds-largest-publicly-available-ml-cluster">As of May 11, 2022</a>, Google's <a target="_blank" href="https://cloud.google.com/tpu">Cloud TPU</a> (Tensor Processing Units) use 2048-chip and 1024-chip <a target="_blank" href="https://analyticsindiamag.com/google-releases-cloud-tpu-v4-pods-benchmarks-for-large-model-training/">v4 Pods</a> which combine for 9 exaflops of peak aggregate performance (equivalent to the computing power of 10 million laptops combined) -- the largest publicly available. Google is also working on <a target="_blank" href="https://blog.google/technology/ai/unveiling-our-new-quantum-ai-campus/">Quantum AI</a>. to run ML hub.

See <a target="_blank" href="
   https://cloud.google.com/why-google">
   https://cloud.google.com/why-google</a>


### As with other clouds

* It's costly and difficult for individual companies to keep up with the pace of technology, especially around hardware
* "Pay as you go" rather than significant up-front purchase, which eats time
* No software to install (and go stale, requiring redo work)
* Google scale - 9 cloud regions in 27 zones. 90 Edge cache locations.

<a target="_blank" href="https://cloud.google.com/about/locations">cloud.google.com/about/locations</a>
lists current number of regions, zones, network edge locations, countries served
by the Google Front End (GFE) with DoS protection.

Google was the first CSP to get an ISO 14001 certification.

2.  <a target="_blank" href="https://www.coursera.org/learn/developing-a-google-sre-culture?specialization=sre-devops-engineer-google-cloud">Developing a Google SRE Culture</a>

    In many IT organizations, incentives are not aligned between developers, who strive for agility, and operators, who focus on stability. Site reliability engineering, or SRE, is how Google aligns incentives between development and operations and does mission-critical production support. Adoption of SRE cultural and technical practices can help improve collaboration between the business and IT. This course introduces key practices of Google SRE and the important role IT and business leaders play in the success of SRE organizational adoption.

    Primary audience: IT leaders and business leaders who are interested in embracing SRE philosophy. Roles include, but are not limited to CTO, IT director/manager, engineering VP/director/manager. Secondary audience: Other product and IT roles such as operations managers or engineers, software engineers, service managers, or product managers may also find this content useful as an introduction to SRE.

3.  <a target="_blank" href="https://www.coursera.org/learn/cloud-infrastructure-design-process?specialization=sre-devops-engineer-google-cloud">Reliable Google Cloud Infrastructure: Design and Process</a> by Stephanie Wong (Developer Advocate) and Philipp Mair (Course Developer)

    equips students to build highly reliable and efficient solutions on Google Cloud using proven design patterns. It is a continuation of the Architecting with Google Compute Engine or Architecting with GKE courses and assumes hands-on experience with the technologies covered in either of those courses. Through a combination of presentations, design activities, and hands-on labs, participants learn to define and balance business and technical requirements to design Google Cloud deployments that are highly reliable, highly available, secure, and cost-effective.

    * Apply a tool set of questions, techniques, and design considerations 
    * Define application requirements and express them objectively as KPIs, SLOs and SLIs

### IAM Permissions

Permissions determine what operations (verbs) are allowed on a resource,
in the form of:

   <ul><pre>service.resource.verb</pre></ul>

The caller of each Google Cloud service REST API method needs to be granted permission for the verb associated with the method called.
For example, the pubsub service:

   <ul><pre>pubsub.subscriptions.consume to call subscriptions.consume()
   pubsub.topics.publish to call topics.publish()</pre></ul>

Permission to access a resource are NOT granted directly to an end user. 


### Permissions by role

Permissions are grouped into <strong>roles</strong> granted to authenticated <a href="#Principals">principals</a>.

Each role is specified in the form of:

   <ul><pre>roles/service.roleName</pre></ul>
   
For example, roles defined for the Cloud Storage service include:

   <ul><pre>roles/storage.objectAdmin
roles/storage.objectCreator
roles/storage.objectViewer</pre></ul>

Again, each role contains a collection of permissions. 

When a role is granted to a principal, that user obtain all the permissions of that role. 

### Allow/IAM Policies by resource

When an authenticated principal attempts to access a resource, IAM determines whether the action is permitted based on the <strong>allow policies</strong> (aka IAM policy) attached to that resource to enforce what roles are granted to which principals.

<a name="RoleBindings"></a>

### Role Bindings

Each allow/IAM policy is a collection of <a href="#RoleBindings">role bindings</a> that bind one or more member principals to an individual role. To define who (principal) has what type of access (role) on a resource, create an allow policy and attach it to the resource.

   * <a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-kZ_eNtxyU">VIDEO</a>: GCP IAM Policies are defined as <strong>bindings to roles</strong>.

### Google's Compute Engines

* <a href="#GCE">Compute Engine</a> (GCE) is a managed environment for deploying <strong>virtual machines (VMs)</strong>, providing full control of VMs for Linux and Windows Server. * <a href="#GCF">Google Cloud Functions</a> (previously called Firebase, like Amazon Lambda) is a managed serverless platform for deploying event-driven functions. It runs single-purpose microservices written in JavaScript executed in Node.js when triggered by events. Good for stateless computation which reacts to external events.

GAE is a managed platform for Google to <strong>deploy and host full app code</strong> at scale. Similar to Amanzon Beanstalk, GAE runs full Go, PHP, Java, Python, Node.js, .NET C#, Ruby, etc. coded with login forms and authentication logic. GAE is a "serverless" PaaS (Platform as a Service) platform where Google manages Docker containers containing <strong>web application</strong> 
that responds to <strong>HTTP requests</strong> (Jetty 8, Servlet 3.1, .Net Core, NodeJs, etc.).

LANGUAGES: Develop server-side code in Java, Python, Go, PHP, NodeJs, .NET, Ruby.

Google provides app versioning - split traffic between different application versions to perform A/B testing.

### ACLs

<a name="CSEK"></a>

## CSEK

Each chunk is distributed across Google's storage infra.
All chunks (sub-files) within an object are encrypted at rest with their own unique Data Encryption Key (DEK). 

DEKs are wrapped by KEKs (Key Encryption Keys) stored in KMS.

With Google-manged keys: The standard key rotation period is 90 days, storing 20 versions.
Re-encryption after 5 years.

Customer-managed keys: Keys are in a key ring.

Customer-supplied keys are stored outside of GCP.

LAB: 
Create an encryption key and wrap it with the Google Compute Engine RSA public key certificate

1. create a 256 bit (32 byte) random number to use as a key:

   openssl rand 32 > mykey.txt
   more mykey.txt

   Result:

   Qe7>hk=c}

1. Download the GCE RSA public cert:

   curl \
   https://cloud-certs.storage.googleapis.com/google-cloud-csek-ingress.pem \
   > gce-cert.pem

Use a RSA public key to encrypt your data. After that data has been encrypted with the public key, it can only be decrypted by the respective private key. In this case, the private key is known only to Google Cloud Platform services. Wrapping your key using the RSA certificate ensures only Google Cloud Platform services can unwrap your key and use it to protect your data.

1. Extract the public key from the certificate:

   <pre>openssl x509 -pubkey -noout -in gce-cert.pem > pubkey.pem</[re>

1. RSA-wrap your key:

   <pre>openssl rsautl -oaep -encrypt -pubin -inkey pubkey.pem -in \
   mykey.txt -out rsawrappedkey.txt</pre>

1. Encode in base64 in base64:

   <pre>openssl enc -base64 -in rsawrappedkey.txt | tr -d '\n' | sed -e \
   '$a\' > rsawrapencodedkey.txt</pre>

1. View your encoded, wrapped key to verify it was created:

   <pre>cat rsawrapencodedkey.txt</pre>

1. To avoid introducing newlines, use code editor to copy the contents rsawrapencodedkey.txt

   <pre>MBMCbcFk ... h4eiqQ==</pre>

1. PROTIP: Click the "X" at the right to exit the Editor.

   ### Encrypt a new persistent disk with your own key

   <a name="AttachDisk"></a>

   ### Attach the disk to a compute engine instance

1. In the browser tab showing the GCP console, select Navigation menu > Compute Engine > VM Instances. Click the Create button. Name the instance csek-demo and verify the region is us-central1 and the zone is us-central1-a.

1. Scroll down and expand Management, security disks, networking, sole tenancy.
Click on Disks and under Additional disks, click Attach existing disk.
For the Disk property, select the encrypted-disk-1.
Paste the value of your wrapped, encoded key into the Enter Key field, and check the Wrapped key checkbox. (You should still have this value in your clipboard).
Leave the Mode as Read/write and Deletion rule as keep disk.

1. Click the Create button to launch the new VM. The VM will be launched and have 2 disks attached. The boot disk and the encrypted disk. The encrypted disk still needs to be formatted and mounted to the instance operating system.

   Important. Notice the encryption key was needed to mount the disk to an instance. Google does not store your keys on its servers and cannot access your protected data unless you provide the key. This also means that if you forget or lose your key there is no way for Google to recover the key or to recover any data encrypted with the lost key.

1. Once the instance has been booted, click the SSH button to connect to the instance.

1. Issue the following commands on the instance to format and mount the encrypted volume:

   <pre>sudo mkfs.ext4 /dev/disk/by-id/google-encrypted-disk-1
   mkdir encrypted
   sudo mount /dev/disk/by-id/google-encrypted-disk-1 encrypted/
   </pre>

   The disk is now mounted as the encrypted folder and can be used like any other disk.
