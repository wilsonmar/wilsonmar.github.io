## Your data is exposed

Today, what used to be secrets that uniquely identify you (social security number, birth date, home address, etc.) are now exposed over the dark web.

Even the government agency (DISA) which processes applications for Top-Secret clearance for all branches of the US military has been hacked.

Yet, many companies still give anyone access to your information when given enough of your secrets.

Monitoring is not enough. What will you do if you find out if you've been exposed?

## Proactive defense

Here's what can you do to guard your secrets:
I can help you.

   1. Avoid doing business with <a target="_blank" href="https://haveibeenpwned.com/PwnedWebsites">organizations which have been hacked</a> or does not offer MFA.

   1. On your mac, install and use a <a target="_blank" href="https://www.pcmag.com/picks/stop-trackers-dead-the-best-private-browsers">secure internet browser</a>: <a target="_blank" href="https://torproject.org/">Tor</a> using <a target="_blank" href="https://formulae.brew.sh/cask/tor-browser"><tt>brew install --cask tor-browser</tt></a>
   1. Install a Password Manager/Generator (KeePassXC) to generate a new for each account.
   1. Create a password database that works locally rather than over the internet cloud.
   1. Store your password vault on a secure USB device.
   1. Enable your trustee to access them if you are ever disabled or killed.

   1. Install <a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JHIAIzOPz3I">Authenticator App</a> <a target="_blank" href="https://getaegis.app/">Aegis (Android)</a> or Authy on your mobile phone for MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication).
   1. Get a USB Yubikey (with custom markings) on your physical keychain to protect key accounts (emails, Paypal, banking, etc.).

   1. Create a different email for signing up to utilities (phone, trash, electric, etc.).
   1. <a href="Phone">Setup the phone number from your mobile carrier for Google Voice</a>
   1. <a href="Phone">Give out your Google number rather than the phone number from your mobile carrier</a>.
   1. Note in your Password Manager every account where you gave your private phone, so you can update them when that changes.

   1. Generate random nonsense words as answers to account recovery, and write them in your Password Manager.
   1. Give to medical personnel a birthdate that is a few days off because you'll be asked to announce it publicly at medical offices.
   1. Do not give out information to somone calling you.

   1. <a href="#LockSSN">Block thieves from using your social security number to apply for jobs</a>.

   1. Open a POBOX to receive physical mail so your home address is not exposed.

   1. Create a different name and each account for social media.

### UnBlock Credit

If you forget to unfreeze you credit before applying, you will most likely be denied.

Credit agencies must lift the freeze within one hour of an unfreeze request made online or by phone,
or within three business days when made by mail.

* https://my.equifax.com/membercenter/#/home

* https://service.transunion.com/dss/login.page?dest=freezeStatus


### Free credit report

[_] Mark on your calendar a repeating appointment each year to request a credit report from each credit reporting agency.

According to https://consumer.ftc.gov/free-credit-reports
Extra free reports qualify if denied credit/employment, suspecting fraud, or unemployed.

1. In a browser, go to <a target="_blank" href="https://www.AnnualCreditReport.com">AnnualCreditReport.com</a> and select which bureau's report you want (or all three).

   WARNING: Other sites offering this service are scams. https://consumer.ftc.gov/free-credit-reports

2. Verify your identity with details like name, address, SSN, and account info—no credit card needed.

3. View reports instantly online, or request by phone (1-877-322-8228) or mail (form to P.O. Box 105281, Atlanta, GA 30348-5281; expect 15 days).
