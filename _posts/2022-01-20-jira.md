## Conceptual Hierarchy

![jira-hierarchy-1200x624](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/300046/151562271-31a365b5-00be-40b5-916a-8b86b2e47de0.png)

<img align="right" width="238" alt="jira-hierarchy-476x434" src="https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/300046/151556443-74d81f71-8e93-436c-82ca-869c4211615f.png">

* <strong>Epics</strong> are large bodies of work that can be broken down into a number of a series of related and interdependent stories. Epics are shown in the <strong>Roadmap</strong>. Most teams complete two or three epics  each calendar quarter.

* <a href="#Initiatives"><strong>Initiatives</strong></a> are collections of epics that drive toward a common goal.
Initiatives often are completed in multiple quarters within a year.
   
   <a target="_blank" href="https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/300046/151562467-8c0ba924-b459-42f8-a9db-b0c99a9f4aac.png"><img alt="jira-initiative-screen-1693x532" src="https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/300046/151562467-8c0ba924-b459-42f8-a9db-b0c99a9f4aac.png"></a>

* <strong>Stories</strong>, also called “user stories,” are short requirements or requests written from the perspective of an end user. Ideally, each story is scoped so the team can commit to finish within a one- or two-week sprint. Stories are listed in the <strong>Backlog</strong>.

* <strong>Tasks</strong> are assignments to fulfill a particular story.

* <strong>Subtasks</strong> track small pieces of work that are part of a larger task.

REMEMBER: Each of the above are all entered into Jira as an "issue" (a "record" or "object" in the Jira database).

PROTIP: Jira has a different usage for the word "issues" than in GitHub.

Use of initiatives require the "ADVANCED ROADMAPS FOR JIRA" Jira plug-in installed ( JIRA Server/Datacenter deployment), previously called "portfolio for JIRA".

You can manage this one of two ways:

1. From the roadmap you can perform this by checking the box for scope next to your initiative, then click bulk actions > parent > review changes > save to jira and it will connect your epics to your initiative. 

2. Add the parent link field to your screens, so that you can see and directly link/unlink epics from initiatives.

An administrator with global permission in Jira Software creates the initiative issue type
In <strong>Advanced Roadmaps</strong>. 

The initiative hierarchy level is not included in the default hierarchy level in Advanced Roadmaps. 
With initiatives being the high-level priorities that multiple teams work against in an organization, it's essential to use the initiative hierarchy level when planning work in Advanced Roadmaps.

## New Jira user

Unless your enterprise admin may has created another mechanism, here is the default way to grant access to new users:

1. Click "Project settings" on the left menu. If you don't see the option you don't have admin. permissions.
1. Click "Access".
1. Click the blue "Add people" button.
1. In the pop-up, type a email address (you can also type a name, but an email address is most accurate).
1. Click Add.


## JIRA workflow

* <strong>Project</strong>. Issues are created with a project prefix.

* <a href="#Bugs">Bug</a> (defect) is an issue which describes a problem which impairs or prevents functioning of the product. A bug is usually specific to a single or related set of system components.

* <strong>Issue</strong> in JIRA is a collective term for user stories and tasks. Usage of the term is different than the societal connotation "that person has issues" which describes a problem condition, such as how a component or workflow is "broken" (not optimal). 

* Improvement describes an enhancement to existing feature or task.

* <strong>Epic</strong> describes a general use case. Best to begin with a product name. Each epic is linked to a collection of user stories (product features) and Engineering Tasks that the team needs to do (such as "build QA server", "schema change", "dependency version upgrade", etc.).

   Epic links in JIRA are selected by name, not code name.

* <strong>User Story</strong> describes a feature of the product from the end-user's point of view. Examples are "Login", "Password recovery". These do not begin with an action verb. A story is often written using a standard format: "As a <em>role</em>, I want <em>feature</em> so that <em>reason/benefit</a>." User stories are either a state of InProgress or Done.

* <strong>Sub-Task</strong> describes development tasks to accomplish a specific user story. These begin with a verb such as "Design", "Create", etc. 
No story point estimates are made on these. Generally no more than 1-day tasks. 
The status of sub-tasks move from TODO to InProgress to QA Ready to Done.
Sub-Tasks do not appear in the <strong>Backlog</strong>.

* <strong>Task</strong> describes engineering work that are not demonstrated to end-users in relation to a user story that end-users experience. Tasks are not estimated in teams that use story points to designate business value rather than effort.<a target="_blank" title="Epic vs Story vs Task In Jira, Jun 12, 2019" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fLNtFJkj10&time=7m2s">*</a>
