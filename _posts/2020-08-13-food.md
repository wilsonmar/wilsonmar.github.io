## Potassium

kidney.com notes that I can protect my kidneys by watching how much I consume potassium.

A potassium level of 5 or higher detected in the blood, called hyperkalemia,
means the kidneys are not able to remove excess potassium. That can cause irregular heartbeat or a heart attack.

REMEMBER: <strong>5175 mg/day</strong> is the basis for percentages of daily on US food labels. <a target="_blank" href="https://louisville.edu/medicine/departments/familymedicine/files/Potassium%20Food%20List.pdf">1 Potassium point</a> = 39mg for 132 points/day.

### Cooking eggs

To cook hard-boiled eggs, <a target="_blank" href="https://www.seriouseats.com/steamed-hard-boiled-eggs-recipe">steam them</a> ensures uniform cooking, preventing rubbery whites. Using a steamer removes the guesswork of timing that comes with boiling. In a steamer basket, cover, and cook <strong>6 minutes for soft-boiled eggs or 12 minutes for hard-boiled</strong>. Alternative: air fryer 8 - 12 eggs at 275 for 12 mins.

Rapid cooling in an ice bath halts the cooking process, preserving the ideal texture and preventing overcooking.

Remove shell under a thin stream of running water. (The water helps get under the shell and lift it off the egg.)

## Sugar & Carbs: Glucose 

Our pancreas produces insulin that travels through our blood to break down sugar. So low insulin is called Diabetes disease.

Our bodies convert carbohydrates to sugar.

The "Hemoglobin A1C (HbA1c)" blood test measures the average blood sugar (glucose) based on the percentage of hemoglobin (a protein in red blood cells) that is coated with sugar, known as glycated hemoglobin. levels over the past 2 to 3 months. 

   * Normal: below 5.7%
   * Prediabetes: 5.7% to 6.4%
   * Diabetes: 6.5% or above
   <br /><br />

A1C of 7% roughly corresponds to an average blood sugar level of 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L).

The glucose test at the doctor's office identified a calibrated level that is <strong>10 points above</strong> what my Stelo Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM) reported at the same time. 

So when the CGM identified a baseline of 104 mg/dL (milligrams per deciliter)after fasting (waking up after 8 hours sleep), I'm realy at 94 mg/dL, which is within normal glucose levels for a healthy adult of between 70 and 99 mg/dL. A fasting level higher than 126 mg/dL suggests diabetes.

The Stelo CGM issues a notification to my phone above a set threshold. That enables me to see to what level different foods spike my blood glucose level:

   * A can of peaches took me to <strong>140</strong> mg/dL.
   <br /><br />

Sustained high readings above 140 to 199 mg/dL (7.8 mmol/L and 11.0 mmol/L) suggests prediabetes. Levels above 200 mg/dL or 11.1 mmol/L (millimoles per liter) suggests diabetes. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/diabetes/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20371451">*</a>

Normally, two hours after eating, levels should be back to baseline.

REMEMBER: Eating protein before sugar intake and exercise after eating helps to reduce levels.

The pancreas does not differentiate between natural vs. artificial sugar (Sucralose).

## Meats

Many wild game meats (venison, pheasant, quail, rabbit, etc.) are known to be high in purines which cause gout inflammation.

That's also the case with organ meats ("sweetbreads") such as liver, kidney, tongue, heart.

Salmon seems to be an exception to fish with high purine levels, in that most gout sufferers don’t have a problem with it.
