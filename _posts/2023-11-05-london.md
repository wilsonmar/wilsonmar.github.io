## The Oldest

For Americans, part of the fun of London is the old stuff.

Romans created the city of London.

<a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/tosCNq496yU">VIDEO</a>:
The Guildhall building is the seat of the City of London Corporation --
the world's oldest continuous city democracy.
The Potions scene in the film "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets" was filmed in its dungeon.
But it's not open to the public now.

<a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/j9M0s-sJU_0">VIDEO</a>:
The oldest parish church in London is St. Bartholomew's Church (called St. Barts).
Built in 1123, the ground under it has risen such that the benches outside it is higher than its entry door. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/K3VooLNnMjw">VIDEO</a>
Inside the church is a modern golden statue by Damien Hurst depicting Bartholow, an apostle of Jesus.
He holds his own skin from being flayed alive.

Across the park from it is a residence that survived the <a target="_blank" href="https://www.historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/HistoryofEngland/The-Great-Fire-of-London/">Great Fire of September 2-5, 1666</a>, German air raid bombing during World War II, and urban revitalizations.
<a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/bBh-DxWmzik">VIDEO</a>:
41-42 Cloth Fair in Smithfield is what some say is the oldest house in London.
Being built in 1614, it survived the Great Fire which burned 87 parish churches, and about 13,000 houses.

It also survived the slum clearance of the 1920s because architects of the slum clearance purchased it.
But what makes this special is that famous visitors (Winston Churchill, the Queen Mother, etc.)
had the foresight to give a metal pen to famous visitors to write a note on the glass window on the 2nd floor.

The Shakespeare's Globe theater on the banks of the Thames river is a reconstruction of the original Globe theater in London. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/nxZLKnHyiIQ">VIDEO</a>:
Shakespeare made up 800 new words</a> that are still in use everyday.

A <strong>plint</strong> is a horizontal band of stone or brick, where statues are erected.
https://www.youtube.com/shorts/nxZLKnHyiIQ

<a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/7oing7dhudQ">VIDEO</a>:
For a Victorian/Edwardian era atmosphere, take a walk in Bloomsbury and Gray's Inn Road.
Charles Dickens lived on Doughty Street.
At night, Green Park is lit with gas street lamps from those days.
